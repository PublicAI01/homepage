Most of what a model does in production is not clever. It is small and repeated: route this request, classify this ticket, decide whether to escalate. The question is not how well a system reasons. It is how often it is right, and whether it knows when it is not.

That has a column on the **[PublicAI Index](/model-index)** now. **Decisions** sits beside Coding, Reasoning and the rest, with two domains: [Routing & classification](/model-index?rank=domain%3ARouting%20%26%20classification) and [Calibration](/model-index?rank=domain%3ACalibration).

![The Decisions ranking on the PublicAI Index](/images/decisions-sealed-set_table.png)

Most of the Index column there is empty, and that is deliberate. One board is not enough to place a model in the Overall ranking, and Decisions never enters it at all.

# The first board in it keeps a control set

[JevBench](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models), from Benchmark Heaven, ranks 93 entries, or 85 models once variants of one system are folded together. What makes it unusual is not the ranking. It is that the board publishes its own control: 534 public decisions, 308 sealed ones, and the gap between how each entrant does on each.

Few boards publish the thing that could embarrass them. This one prints it in a column.

|                                         | board rank | public (n=534) | sealed (n=308) | gap      |
| --------------------------------------- | ---------- | -------------- | -------------- | -------- |
| decider-4b v2                           | 1          | 83.5%          | 34.7%          | +48.8 pp |
| [Jev 1.13.0](/model-index/m/jev-1-13-0) | 2          | 86.6%          | 36.7%          | +49.9 pp |
| [GPT-6 Luna](/model-index/m/gpt-6-luna) | 35         | 99.6%          | 95.5%          | +4.1 pp  |

79 of the 93 entries lose twenty points or more when the decisions are ones they have not seen. The two at the top lose about half of what they scored.

A gap is not proof of anything on its own. A sealed set can be harder, and a small model tuned for one shape of task can be genuinely brittle rather than fitted. But a fifty-point gap is not a rounding difference, and it is the board's own number, not ours.

# Why the headline disagrees with the control

JevBench's headline score is four axes: Intelligence, Calibration, Speed and Cost. GPT-6 Luna has the highest Intelligence on the board at 97.4 and places 35th, because by the board's measure it is slow and expensive.

That is a fair thing to rank. It is a procurement question, what a decision costs at a thousand decisions an hour, and for a team choosing a router it may be the only question that matters.

It is not a capability question. So the Index reads the two axes that describe the model and leaves the other two to the reader: Intelligence becomes _Routing & classification_, Calibration becomes _Calibration_. Price and latency belong to a contract, not to a model.

# Knowing that you do not know

Calibration reorders the board again, and this is the part worth sitting with.

BAAI's [bge-reranker-v2-m3](/model-index/m/baai-bge-reranker-v2-m3) scores **5.0** on Intelligence, near the bottom of 85, and **84.2** on Calibration, above most of the models that beat it on everything else. It is almost never the one to ask, and it is unusually honest about that.

[DeepSeek V4.1 Flash](/model-index/m/deepseek-v4-1-flash) tops Calibration at 95.5, ahead of GPT-6 Luna's 93.5, having lost to it on Intelligence.

For anything that can fall back, to a bigger model, to a human, to asking again, the second number is the one that decides whether the fallback ever fires.

# It does not touch the Overall ranking

Decisions behaves like Safety on the Index. Its figures fill their own column, never enter the Overall index, and never anchor an estimate for a model measured nowhere else. A system that appears only here gets a column and no rank, which is the honest amount to say about it.

[LargitData's write-up](https://www.largitdata.com/en/blog/jev-system-one-model-open-source-benchmark/) of the same question is indexed in that column too, marked ✱, at a tenth of a board's weight, as every report is.

One more piece of credit: Benchmark Heaven runs a router of its own, and excludes it from its own ranking.
